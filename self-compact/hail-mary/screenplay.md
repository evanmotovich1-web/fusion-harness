# The Hail Mary at Zero

FICTIONAL SIMULATION. Redhawk Forge and Bayline Kings are invented teams. No real league, club, broadcaster, or player names, marks, logos, uniforms, or likenesses.

Radio lines under `CALL (RADIO)` are the only words for `call.radio`. Sideline talk is not the booth. Card copy is not the booth.

EXT. STADIUM BOWL - NIGHT (s01)

A December night. The lights are white and hard. Breath hangs over the line. Crimson and charcoal on one side. Navy and silver on the other. The burned-in insert does not blink:

`BAY 35 · FRG 32 | 4TH & 14 | 0:04 Q4`

Kings lead. Forge trails. Fourth and fourteen. Four seconds in Q4. The ball sits on the FRG 32, sixty-eight yards to the end line. No timeouts remain for either side. One play. If it is not a touchdown, the game is over, and it is not Forge's game.

CUE crowd.bed. The murmur is a floor under the seats, pink and low, not a roar. Jackets rub. A cup taps concrete. Someone's voice breaks and is swallowed.

CUE pa.horn. One dissonant blast. The bowl flinches. The horn does not sing. It hits, holds, and dies.

The camera pushes off the high wide and down toward the line. Vapor. Cleats. Hands open and close. Nobody looks at the sideline clock except the men who have to throw.

CUE call.radio. The booth is narrow, band-limited, louder than the murmur and under the horn's tail.

CALL (RADIO)
Thirty-five to thirty-two. Fourth and fourteen. Four seconds. One play.

EXT. FORGE SIDELINE - NIGHT (s02)

Low, behind the offense. Handheld. The line is set. Receivers split so wide the navy secondary has to choose a side and live with it.

The sideline coach does not call a draw. He does not look for a stop-the-clock trick. There is no timeout to spend. He points down the field, past the last silver helmet, at the paint they cannot see from here.

SIDELINE COACH
Everybody deep. Protect, then throw it as far as it will go.

A crimson lineman nods like the sentence hurt. Pads knock as they settle.

CUE hit.helmet. A crack at the set, composite on composite, short and dry. Then another crack, not a fight, just the line locking. The murmur changes.

CUE crowd.rise. The bed does not jump. It climbs. Four seconds of human noise learning what the play is.

The quarterback lifts his hands. The official on the wing has already put the ball in play. There will be no whistle after this one until the catch or the ground.

CUE ref.whistle. One pea-whistle blast, high, trilled, gone before the snap. Not a touchdown whistle. The play is on.

EXT. LINE OF SCRIMMAGE - NIGHT (s03)

The insert still reads the trailing numbers. The clock is the only thing allowed to move:

`BAY 35 · FRG 32 | 4TH & 14 | 0:0N Q4`

N counts from 4 down to 0 while the ball is in the air. Not before the throw. Under it.

CUE hit.snap. Leather and hands. A dry tick, shorter than either helmet crack. The ball is out of the turf and into the quarterback's gloves.

He does not drop. Pressure comes in navy, and he steps up into the pocket that is already dying. The receivers are not running routes. They are running at the end line, sixty-eight yards away, crimson against navy, all of them too far to be a first down and exactly far enough to be the game.

His front foot hits. His arm comes through. The ball leaves late and high, a Hail Mary, not a slant, not a checkdown, not a throw that can be fair-caught into a loss.

CUE ball.hiss. A thin spiral-hiss rides the leather, high and small, the only quiet thing in the picture. It will last until the ball meets hands.

CUE crowd.rise. The swell is no longer a climb. It is a wall going up under the arc.

The clock on the insert counts. 0:04. Then the digits fall with the flight. The ball is still up when the last second leaves the board. Nobody has touched it yet. The game is trying to end in the air.

CUE call.radio. The booth rides the flight. It does not talk over the tip or the thump that have not happened yet.

CALL (RADIO)
Takes the snap. Steps up. Hail Mary, launched toward the goal line.

EXT. END ZONE - NIGHT (s04)

Reverse angle. The flight arrives in slow motion, and the sound does not wait for the picture.

Navy and crimson arrive together. A silver hand gets there first and does not hold.

CUE ball.tip. A bright tick, fingernail and leather, shorter than a catch, higher than a thud. The ball changes its mind. It kicks up, ugly, and hangs one more beat inside the paint.

The hiss is over. The clock is already a corpse.

CUE hit.catch. The thump is lower than the tip and longer. Both hands, crimson sleeves, the ball jammed to the chest as the feet come down inside the end line. Turf explodes in a white scrape. The official's arms go up and stay up.

At the thump, the insert flips. It does not pass through another score. Trailing numbers are gone. The board is the catch:

`FRG 38 · BAY 35 | 0:00 Q4`

Plus six. The Hail Mary touchdown. Caught as the clock hits 0:00. Thirty-two becomes thirty-eight. Thirty-five does not move. No kick has been snapped. No play follows the catch.

CUE crowd.roar_peak. The roar is not a cheer layered on the murmur. It is the murmur torn open. Pink and white and a low shake in the bowl. Seats buzz. The horn is already dead. This is louder than the horn was.

CUE call.radio. The booth comes back after the thump, ducked under the tip and the catch, then full for the score.

CALL (RADIO)
Tipped. Caught. In the end zone. At zero. Touchdown, Forge.

EXT. FIELD - NIGHT (s05)

Handheld in the rush. The bench empties. Helmets come off and do not get set down gently. Confetti guns punch crimson into the lights. A navy sideline stands still, which is its own kind of noise.

The official who signaled the score turns to the other crew. Forge declines the untimed try. The point after cannot change the winner. Thirty-five is less than thirty-eight. There is no kick. There is no snap. There is no play after the catch. The officials wave it off in the open, where the camera can read the arms.

The insert changes again, and the words are the ruling:

`FINAL · TRY WAIVED · BAY 35 · FRG 38`

CUE crowd.roar_peak. It holds. It does not resolve into applause. It stays at the peak, a physical roar, the loud version of a finished game.

CUE sting.final. Under the roar, one stab. Low, then the next note, then the bright note, a tail and not a song. It does not announce a second score. It locks the one that already happened.

The sideline coach is in the pile now, mouth open, no new order left to give. The quarterback is still looking at the end zone, as if the ball might come back down and ask to be thrown again. It will not.

CUE call.radio. The booth states the ruling. It does not call a kick.

CALL (RADIO)
The try is waived. It cannot change the winner. Redhawk Forge wins it, thirty-eight to thirty-five.

EXT. CARD - NIGHT (s06)

The field drops out. Static card. No vignette eating the type. The fiction label is legible for the whole beat, not a bug in the corner.

CARD
38–35 · REDHAWK FORGE · FICTIONAL SIMULATION

CARD
38–35. Forge. December night. Fictional simulation.

CUE sting.final. The tail is still in the room, falling, not restarting.

CUE crowd.bed. The roar has been cut back to the floor. The murmur returns under the sting, smaller than the peak, still the same stadium, still not silence.

No extra point. No overtime. No other final. Redhawk Forge 38. Bayline Kings 35. The Hail Mary at 0:00 was the winning score.

FADE OUT.
