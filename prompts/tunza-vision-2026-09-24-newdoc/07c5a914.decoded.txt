WHY KENYA NEEDS THIS LAYER NOW
The system is digitizing quickly. The
decision gap cannot wait.
Kenya is not short of ambition or digital infrastructure. It is simultaneously scaling community health, connecting
facilities, formalizing referral policy, expanding national registries and exchange, strengthening emergency
dispatch, and introducing mandatory certification for digital health systems. The urgency for Tunza comes from
the space between those systems: the point where a person is still outside formal care and the next decision can
determine whether care is timely, appropriate, and reachable.
The human cost is current, not theoretical. WHO's 2025 Kenya report still places maternal mortality at an estimated 355 deaths per
100,000 live births, with preventable causes such as postpartum haemorrhage and hypertensive disorders continuing to contribute
substantially. Kenya also faces a 92% shortage of doctors against estimated need. When clinical capacity is scarce, delays and poor
routing carry real consequences.

THE PROBLEM IS NOT 'NO DIGITAL HEALTH.' IT IS FRAGMENTED DECISION CONTINUITY.
The Ministry of Health's 2026 referral-policy process explicitly identifies fragmentation in referral pathways and calls for better continuity,
digital interoperability, accountability, clearer forward and backward referral pathways, and improved movement of patients, specimens,
and specialist expertise. That is a signal: the country is already naming the system-level problem Tunza is designed to sit inside.
Before care
The patient or CHP still has uncertainty. Information is incomplete. Distance, transport, facility capability and
urgency matter before the hospital ever sees the case.
During handoff
The critical question is not only 'where is the nearest facility?' but 'what is the right level of care, can this facility
handle it, has it acknowledged the case, and what is the next safe action?'
After care
Without an outcome returning to the original episode, the system cannot reliably learn whether the initial decision,
referral destination, or escalation path was correct.
WHY THE WINDOW IS TIME-SENSITIVE
This is an inference, not an official deadline: the next 12-24 months are likely to matter disproportionately because national referral rules,
interoperability expectations, certification requirements, and institutional digital habits are hardening at the same time. A system that earns
clinical trust and integration credibility while these rails are still being shaped can become easier to embed; a later entrant may be forced to
fit decisions already made.
The question is no longer whether Kenya will digitize care. It is whether digitization will merely
record what happened - or help the right decision happen sooner.

HOW TUNZA MAKES DECISIONS WITHOUT TURNING THE MODEL INTO THE SAFETY BOUNDARY
JEV / TypeSafe Decision Framework
JEV is our internal working framework for turning messy real-world clinical input into bounded, auditable
decisions. It is not an external standard. The core idea is TypeSafe decision-making: the model may interpret and
reason inside a controlled contract, but the system - not the model - owns the allowed states, hard safety rules,
provenance, and actions.
1
Typed Encounter Context
Convert incoming facts into explicit typed observations: concept, value, unit, source, observed time, freshness, confidence, and whether the
value is unknown.
2
Deterministic Safety Envelope
Run non-negotiable danger-sign and policy rules before model interpretation. A model cannot downgrade a hard safety condition because it
sounds confident.
3
Bounded ML Interpretation
Use the model for language understanding, structured extraction, missing-information detection, targeted questions, and policy-constrained
reasoning - never unrestricted side effects.
4
TypeSafe Decision Object
Emit only allowed dispositions and reason codes: urgent/emergency, same-day care, routine care, self-care/monitoring, insufficient
information, or system-degraded.
5
Execution Guard
Translate the decision into eligible actions. Requested is not confirmed: referral send, facility acceptance, arrival, completion and outcome
each require authoritative acknowledgement.
6
Outcome + Evaluation Join
Attach downstream facility/diagnostic/outcome evidence back to the original decision so disagreements and failures become evaluation
evidence, not forgotten anecdotes.
THE DECISION CONTRACT
A Tunza decision should be inspectable and replayable. It should carry the disposition, reason codes, deterministic rule hits,
missing-information state, required next action, policy version, model version, prompt/config version, and provenance references used to
reach it. The model should return structured content that the application validates before any user-facing action is allowed.

WHY THIS MATTERS COMPETITIVELY
Most teams can fine-tune a medical model. Far fewer can build a system where every clinical decision is bounded, typed, versioned,
auditable, reversible when dependencies fail, and linked to downstream outcomes. If Tunza gets this right, the model becomes
replaceable while the decision system, safety policy, evaluation evidence, referral reliability, and outcome loop become progressively
harder to reproduce.
The model is an intelligence component. JEV / TypeSafe is the contract that prevents
intelligence from becoming uncontrolled authority.
Internal note: JEV is a working internal name for the TypeSafe decision architecture. The framework should remain implementation-agnostic so the underlying model, routing provider, or external health
system can change without changing the decision contract.

ML EXECUTION: BUY ITERATION SPEED, NOT GPU THEATER
Four H100s as a controlled research
campaign.
The near-term ML plan is not foundation-model pretraining. We start from strong existing base models, build the
evaluation system first, specialize with SFT/QLoRA, compare against locked clinical evals, mine failures,
adjudicate them, and iterate. H100s shorten that loop; they are not the moat.
Baseline
Run untouched candidates against CORE, RED, ABSTAIN, PARITY and locked GOLD sets before changing weights.
SFT / QLoRA
Axolotl training harness; frozen base weights in 4-bit NF4; BF16 compute on H100s; train LoRA adapters rather than full
weights.
Evaluation
Measure urgent sensitivity, undertriage, overtriage, abstention, danger-sign capture, EN/SW parity and Gold
regressions.
Failure loop
Cluster failures, adjudicate clinically important disagreements, improve data, and rerun one explicit hypothesis at a time.
THE 4-DAY H100 CAMPAIGN
DAY 1
Baseline + smoke + A
Verify 4xH100 / CUDA / NCCL / interconnect, validate immutable hashes, run 100-200 smoke steps, then train Candidate A.
DAY 2
Evaluate A
Run the locked suite, compare to baseline, identify regressions, and cluster clinically important failures.
DAY 3
Targeted B
Change one controlled variable based on an explicit hypothesis - e.g. stronger abstention - then train Candidate B.
DAY 4
Same eval, decision
Compare BASELINE vs A vs B on the exact same suite. Reject, send for clinical review, or promote toward shadow mode.
4 H100s x $3.50/hr = $14/hr. 96 hours continuously = $1,344. Do not auto-spend it. Target first campaign: $500-$900, with GPUs
treated as a shared pool and released whenever they are not generating useful training/evaluation work.
GITHUB IS THE CONTROL PLANE; THE GPU NODE IS DISPOSABLE COMPUTE.
Commits run cheap CPU CI. A merged SHA becomes eligible for training; it does not automatically spend GPU money. A deliberate Train
Candidate action freezes Git SHA, model revision, dataset hash, eval version, Axolotl config, seed, max spend and max runtime. Only
then does orchestration provision H100s.

THE COMPOUNDING ADVANTAGE
The H100s buy faster iteration. The durable advantage is the loop: high-quality care-path data -> typed decisions -> locked evals -> failure
analysis -> clinician adjudication -> better SFT -> safer decisions -> better referrals -> outcomes returned -> stronger evidence. As that
loop compounds, Tunza depends less on any single model vendor.
What should feel urgent to Jacob is not 'we need GPUs.' It is that Kenya is formalizing the
digital rails now, while the clinical decision layer between community and formal care is still
open enough to shape.
Internal execution principle: every month should end with something Tunza can prove that it could not prove the month before - not simply more features. Distillation comes later, after we have a
demonstrably better teacher and a clear deployment reason to compress it.
