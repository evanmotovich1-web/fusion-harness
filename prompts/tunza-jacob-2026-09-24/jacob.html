Internal. Do not send.

Tunza has to exist. The demo does not, yet.

Someone is sick. Care is far. The question is who goes first, not what the diagnosis is. eCHIS does not order that queue. The child who waits can die before a facility ever sees them. That gap is current. Tunza is the missing decision before the clinic. It is not built.

Kenya already has the people and is writing the rails. More than 107,000 community health promoters have reached over nine million households. The Ministry is writing a national referral policy. None of that decides, at the household door, which sick child moves now. A policy cannot execute a guess.

The country's death numbers are not Tunza's results. Do not say them that way. The figure in recent reporting is about 355 maternal deaths per 100,000 live births, on the order of 16 mothers a day. The causes named there are bleeding, high blood pressure, obstructed labor, distance, and clinics that cannot do the job. That is the cost of delay and the wrong door. It is not evidence that this app would have saved them.

What you can show if he asks

A phone demo. English and Kiswahili. A household can start. The rules return a next step: go now, get care today, monitor at home, or need one more answer. They are a demonstration. They are not clinically validated. The case stays on that phone. The facility on a referral is fake. The screen says this is not emergency care.

If he asks for the decision system, the trained model, the GPU run, or the outcome loop, say those are not built. Do not name an internal framework. Do not quote a price. Do not say 10,277 facilities or a 92 percent doctor shortfall. Those were not checked. Do not say a circle of clinicians already exists. It does not.

Here is what you do so he feels the window closing

Do not tell him what should feel urgent. Make him picture the seat he loses if he waits.

  1. Open on the child, not the product. One sentence. Someone is sick, care is far, nothing in the current system says which child goes first, and that child can die in the wait. Then stop. Let him sit in it.
  2. Put the clock on the country, not on you. Kenya is writing the referral rules, the certification habits, and the digital rails now. Say this is your read, not a Ministry deadline: the next year or two is when a clinician can still change the safety line. After the rules harden, he inherits someone else's line. He does not get to write it.
  3. Name the thing he loses. Not a logo. Not a model. The first definition of an unacceptable miss. The first rule for when the system must shut up. The evidence a medical director would trust before this touches a real case. Whoever is in the room now writes those. Whoever arrives later annotates them.
  4. Keep the room small, and say so. Tell him the first conversations are few on purpose, because the safety line gets expensive to change once more people have touched it. Do not invent a headcount. Do not say the seats are filling. Say the line gets written with or without him, and the writing has started because the Ministry has started.
  5. Ask where he would distrust it. Not "would you use this." Where would a technically correct path fail in his ward. What miss should stop a pilot cold. What evidence he would require before he let it near a real decision. The man who answers those questions owns the bar. The man who passes reads the bar later.
  6. If he leans in, do not hand him a build plan. Hand him the next hour. One conversation, this week, on one use case and the line that stops a pilot. No term sheet. No GPU. No national rollout. If he asks what a check buys, say that page is not written. Terms are not set.
  7. If he hesitates, do not add features. Repeat the miss. The referral policy is already in motion. The queue is still unordered. The child does not wait for the architecture diagram. Then ask which part of that journey he thinks creates the most avoidable death. Leave with his answer, not with yours.

Do not say Tunza saves lives. Do not say it is the only gap. Do not say the model is the moat. The feeling you want is not admiration. It is that the safety line is being written now, and he is not in the room yet.
